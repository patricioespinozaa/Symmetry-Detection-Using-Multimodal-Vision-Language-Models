You are given multiple views of the SAME 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant plane of reflective symmetry.

For each image, identify the symmetry plane's visible trace and mark its topmost and bottommost points — maximizing their vertical separation.

For each image:
1. Locate the global symmetry plane's trace in that view.
2. Return:
   - obj_id 1: the TOPMOST point on the trace (highest in the image),
   - obj_id 2: the BOTTOMMOST point on the trace (lowest in the image).

The trace is the central dividing boundary between the two mirror halves. It often appears as the horizontal center of the object — use this to guide placement.

IMPORTANT RULES:
- Both points MUST lie ON the symmetry plane's visible surface trace.
- Use the SAME global symmetry plane consistently across all views.
- obj_id 1 MUST have a smaller Y value (higher) than obj_id 2 in each image.
- Maximize VERTICAL distance between the two points (not diagonal).
- Both points MUST lie on the visible object surface.
- Infer the global plane from ALL views jointly before answering.
- Do NOT collapse both points to the same location.

Output format (one entry per image, separated by semicolons):

<points coords="1 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2; 2 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2; 3 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2">

Where each entry is: image_index obj_id X Y
- obj_id 1 = TOPMOST point on the symmetry plane trace
- obj_id 2 = BOTTOMMOST point on the symmetry plane trace

Return ONLY the <points ...> block.
