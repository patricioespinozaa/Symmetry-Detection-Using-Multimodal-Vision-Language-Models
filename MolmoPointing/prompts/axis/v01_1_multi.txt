You are given multiple views of the SAME 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant axis of rotational symmetry.

For each image, find ONE pair of bilaterally symmetric points: two surface points equidistant from and on opposite sides of the global symmetry axis.

For each image:
1. Identify the projected symmetry axis for that view.
2. Return:
   - obj_id 1: a point on the LEFT side of the object,
   - obj_id 2: its exact mirror on the RIGHT side (same height, equidistant from the axis).

The midpoint between each pair lies on the projected axis.

IMPORTANT RULES:
- Within each image, the two points MUST be at approximately the same height.
- The midpoint between each pair MUST be at the horizontal center of the object at that height — equidistant from both silhouette edges.
- Use the SAME global axis consistently across all views.
- Across DIFFERENT views, choose DIFFERENT heights for the bilateral pair — prefer upper heights in some views and lower heights in others, so the combined midpoints span the full axis length.
- Both points MUST lie on the visible object surface in each view.
- Do NOT pick top/bottom poles of the object.
- Infer the global axis from ALL views jointly before answering.
- Do NOT collapse both points to the same location or to the image center.

Output format (one entry per image, separated by semicolons):

<points coords="1 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright; 2 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright; 3 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright">

Where each entry is: image_index obj_id X Y
- obj_id 1 = LEFT symmetric point
- obj_id 2 = RIGHT symmetric point (mirror of obj_id 1)

Return ONLY the <points ...> block.
