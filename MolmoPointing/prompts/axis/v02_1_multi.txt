You are given multiple views of the SAME 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant axis of rotational symmetry.

For each image, identify two points on the axis centerline at geometrically distinct heights: one at the WIDEST visible cross-section and one near the TOP or BOTTOM.

The axis centerline is the horizontal center of the object at each height — equidistant from the left and right silhouette edges.

For each image:
1. Find the widest visible cross-section and locate its horizontal center.
2. Also locate the horizontal center near the top or bottom of the object.
3. Return:
   - obj_id 1: axis centerline point at the widest cross-section height,
   - obj_id 2: axis centerline point near the top or bottom (whichever is farther from obj_id 1).

IMPORTANT RULES:
- Both points in each image MUST be on the axis centerline (NOT on the silhouette boundary).
- Use the SAME global axis consistently across all views.
- The two points MUST be at different heights in each image.
- Infer the global axis from ALL views jointly before answering.
- Do NOT collapse both points to the same position.
- Do NOT place points on the silhouette edges.

Output format (one entry per image, separated by semicolons):

<points coords="1 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2; 2 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2; 3 1 X1 Y1 2 X2 Y2">

Where each entry is: image_index obj_id X Y
- obj_id 1 = axis centerline point at the widest cross-section
- obj_id 2 = axis centerline point near the top or bottom

Return ONLY the <points ...> block.
