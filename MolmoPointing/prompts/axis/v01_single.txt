You are given ONE image of a 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant rotational symmetry axis.

Your task is to find ONE pair of bilaterally symmetric points: two surface points that are exact mirror images of each other about the symmetry axis projection.

Return:
- obj_id 1: a point on the LEFT side of the object
- obj_id 2: its exact mirror point on the RIGHT side (same height, equidistant from the axis)

The midpoint between these two points lies on the projected symmetry axis.

IMPORTANT RULES:
- The two points MUST be at approximately the same height (same Y coordinate).
- The two points MUST be equidistant from the symmetry axis.
- Both points MUST lie on the visible object surface.
- Choose a height that is clearly visible and geometrically well-defined.
- Do NOT pick the topmost or bottommost pole of the object.
- Do NOT collapse both points to the same location or to the image center.

Output ONLY:
