You are given multiple views of the SAME 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant axis of rotational symmetry.

For each image, find ONE pair of bilaterally symmetric points: two surface points that are exact mirror images of each other about the global symmetry axis.

For each image:
1. Identify the projected symmetry axis for that view.
2. Return:
   - obj_id 1: a point on the LEFT side of the object in that view,
   - obj_id 2: its exact mirror point on the RIGHT side (same height, equidistant from the axis).

The midpoint between each pair lies on the projected axis in that view.

IMPORTANT RULES:
- Within each image, the two points MUST be at approximately the same height (same Y coordinate).
- The two points MUST be equidistant from the axis projection.
- Use the SAME global axis consistently across all views.
- Both points MUST lie on the visible object surface.
- Do NOT pick the top or bottom pole of the object.
- Infer the global axis from ALL views jointly before answering.
- Do NOT collapse both points to the same location or to the image center.

Output format (one entry per image, separated by semicolons):

<points coords="1 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright; 2 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright; 3 1 Xleft Yleft 2 Xright Yright">

Where each entry is: image_index obj_id X Y
- obj_id 1 = LEFT symmetric point
- obj_id 2 = RIGHT symmetric point (mirror of obj_id 1)

Return ONLY the <points ...> block.
