You are given multiple views of the SAME 3D object.

The object has ONE dominant axis of rotational symmetry.

For each image, identify the NARROWEST visible cross-section of the object — the "neck" or "waist" where the object's width, measured perpendicular to the axis, is smallest — using the object's global 3D geometry (inferred jointly from all views), not just what looks narrow in a single view.

For each image:
1. Locate the height of the global narrowest cross-section (the neck/waist) as it appears in THIS view, plus the TOP or BOTTOM of the object (whichever gives greater vertical separation).
2. Return:
   - obj_id 1: a point on the axis centerline at the neck/waist height,
   - obj_id 2: a point on the axis centerline at the TOP or BOTTOM (whichever is farther from obj_id 1).

IMPORTANT RULES:
- Both points MUST be on the axis centerline (horizontal center of the object at that height), NOT on the lateral silhouette.
- The neck/waist is a FIXED physical feature of the object — use the SAME neck/waist height consistently across all views, not whatever looks narrowest from each individual angle.
- If the object has no clear neck/waist (e.g. a uniform cylinder or a sphere), use the midpoint of the object's visible vertical extent instead for obj_id 1, consistently across views.
- Infer the global axis and the global neck/waist location from ALL views jointly before answering.
- obj_id 1 and obj_id 2 MUST be at different heights in each image.
- Do NOT place points on the silhouette boundary.

Output format (one entry per image, separated by semicolons):

<points coords="1 1 Xneck Yneck 2 Xend Yend; 2 1 Xneck Yneck 2 Xend Yend; 3 1 Xneck Yneck 2 Xend Yend">

Where each entry is: image_index obj_id X Y
- obj_id 1 = point on the axis centerline at the neck/waist (narrowest cross-section)
- obj_id 2 = point on the axis centerline at the farther of the TOP/BOTTOM

Return ONLY the <points ...> block.
